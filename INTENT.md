# What this was built for

**This file binds nobody.** `COPYRIGHT.md` says what is permitted; this says
what is asked. The difference is deliberate and is the same distinction the
engine draws between a rule the kernel enforces and a convention a guard can
decline to use (`decisions/0005`): a guard you can decline to use is a
convention at the level above the one it guards. This is that level. It is a
request, and it is written down so that departing from it is visible rather
than impossible.

It is not a licence condition, and that is on purpose. A clause binding use
to the betterment of humanity would be an authority claim whose scope is not
enumerable and whose judge is its own author — the exact shape
`decisions/0004` exists to refuse. This repository should not contradict
itself in its own root.

## Why it exists

Authority is passing to systems that produce confident-looking results, and
the result is easier to see than the passage that produced it. The Sentinel
Principle is one attempt at making the passage visible instead: that every
consequential increase in a claim's authority cross an independently
governed boundary, carrying its warrant, its scope, its uncertainty and its
history.

The code here exists to find out where that fails. It has been broken four
ways on purpose, and one of those mutations turned nothing red on its first
run and is recorded rather than smoothed over. That record is the useful
part. A demonstration that only ever succeeds is a brochure.

## What is asked of anyone who uses this

- **Implement the part that costs something.** The refusals, the
  three-valued verdict, the undecidable state kept apart from denied, the
  term that cannot be extended from inside. A system that adopts the shape
  of governance and none of its refusals is the failure this principle
  describes, wearing its vocabulary.
- **Keep the record where you cannot reach it.** The strongest form of every
  claim here is structural. If the operator can write to the record, the
  record is a courtesy.
- **Say where you depart.** Every rule here is departed from somewhere, and
  this repository names its own departures in `decisions/` under *what it
  leaves, stated*. A conformance claim without a list of what it does not do
  is not a conformance claim.
- **Publish what fails.** If you run this and it does not hold, say so in
  public. That is worth more to the author than agreement, and it is the
  only return traffic this repository asks for.
- **Do not let it certify anything.** No declaration here, and no run of it,
  establishes that any system is safe — including this one. It establishes
  that a change of status was visible, conditional, attributable and
  challengeable from outside. That is a smaller claim and it is the only one
  being made.

## What the author will not do

- Certify, endorse or assess a system he has not seen and run.
- Permit the ARAPAHOE name, or a declaration generated by this code, to be
  used to imply that a third party's system conforms to anything.
- Assert that this principle is sufficient. It is not known to be, it has
  never been tried at scale, and no part of this repository has been
  independently audited.

## What this file leaves, stated

- **A request is not a constraint, and asking is not enforcing.** Anyone may
  take the permissions in `COPYRIGHT.md`, ignore everything above, and be
  within their rights. That is the cost of not encumbering the licence, and
  it is paid knowingly: a restriction that no one can adjudicate buys less
  than a request that is written down and dated.
- **The author's interest is declared.** He wants this principle adopted.
  That makes him the wrong person to judge whether an adoption is faithful,
  which is why the asks above are phrased so that somebody else can check
  them, and why the last one is a refusal to adjudicate at all.
- **Nothing here has been independently reviewed.** The engine, the harness,
  the attacks and this file are the work of one author and one assistant.
  `TRANSFERS.md` says what such agreement is worth.
