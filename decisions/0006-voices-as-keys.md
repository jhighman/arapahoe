# 0006 · Voices as keys: the Sentinel is enrolled

**Decided:** 2026-09-26, by Lex, in writing, against the bench's milestone
reading. Recorded the same day by J. Highman's assistant, from her ruling.
**Status:** decided, and **not built**. Nothing in the engine changed. The
document this decision turns on does not exist yet, and until it does the
hole named in §4 of `TRANSFERS.md` stands exactly as written. A ruling is
not a signature.

## What was decided

A role is a vest. A vest without a fingerprint can be worn by whoever
happens to be running the process — which is the whole of the residue that
has now turned up at `door()`, at the envelope's acceptances, at the canon's
readings, at the reconciler's reclaim rows, and at enrolment itself. So the
Sentinel stops being a role and becomes an **enrolled identity**.

Two layers, the same shape already used for charter-grade acts.

**Genesis, outside the ledger.** A signed Founding Roster, kept beside a
`CHARTER.md` this repository does not yet have. That document is the only
place a vest is issued, and amending it is an authors' act rather than a
runtime write.

**Runtime assertion.** A process may speak as Sentinel only where its
fingerprint matches a row in that roster, and the engine checks **a hash of
the roster** rather than that a file is present. The difference is the point:
a file anyone can drop in is another vest without a fingerprint.

**Two keys, two jobs.** One is enrolled and visible, and is how the Sentinel
proves itself while running. The other is offline, in a vault, not on this
machine and not in this repository; it is the only thing that amends the
roster, and the engine never sees it. Neither author can rewrite the vest
alone, which is the property being bought rather than a precaution.

The roster is also where the authority to place a price or a term will sit.
That policy layer is **not** decided here.

## Why

0004 paid the reach debt by moving the price off the author and onto a voice
enrolled as a classifier. What it left is smaller and worse: enrolment is
unauthenticated, so the operator can enroll its own head of growth and the
record holds the row and cannot say who wrote it. A classifier that anyone
may mint is the author under another name, and the same is true one floor
up of a Sentinel that is only a role.

0005 answered custody by moving the record to a principal the operator
cannot write to. It did not answer identity. The kernel can say a process is
not allowed to write; it cannot say which process is entitled to speak as
the Sentinel. That is a question about who, and a question about who is
answered outside the ledger or not at all — the founding roster is where
every other question of this shape in this work has ended up.

## What this leaves open, stated

- **The roster does not exist.** Deciding its shape closes nothing. The hole
  is open until the document is written and signed, and `TRANSFERS.md` §4
  keeps it open above the four items that describe the answer.
- **The policy layer is owed its own decision.** The Sentinel is a turnstile:
  it checks that a ticket is valid and that a price was paid, and it does not
  print the price list. Who may place a reach or a term, and in what act, is
  a separate file. Until it is written the calculator still accepts a number
  from the air.
- **Mismatch behaviour is not decided.** Whether a process whose fingerprint
  is absent refuses to start, or starts and refuses to speak as Sentinel, and
  whether either leaves a row, is not settled here. A refusal nobody can see
  is the failure this repository already knows by name.
- **Revocation is not addressed.** Nothing here says how a vest is withdrawn,
  what happens to rows written under it before the withdrawal, or whether a
  withdrawn vest binds the past. 0004's revocation rule binds the future
  only; whether that generalises to vests is not decided.
- **Key loss and rotation are not addressed.** A vaulted key that neither
  author can produce is a roster nobody can amend. That is a property, not
  an accident, and it has a cost that is not priced here.
- **The bootstrap is not closed by this.** The engine checks the hash of the
  roster, and the engine is the thing being trusted to check it. This moves
  the root of trust outward — which is what a founding roster is for — and
  does not remove it. The bench's own record says the same of its own
  roster, and this decision claims no more than that.
- **The envelope at a real FFI or IPC boundary is left in the air.** One
  change sometimes removes another. If the two keys do not close it, it
  comes back as its own question.
