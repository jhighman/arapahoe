# 0007 · A second site, without a sentinel

**Decided:** 2026-10-07, by J. Highman.
**Status:** decided. Drafted by the build's assistant and decided the same
day. Nothing in it was run against this repository's code: every claim
about ARAPAHOE is read off its sources, and every claim about the other
repository is read off its commits.

## What was decided

That `github.com/jhighman/milo-and-me` is entered in `TRANSFERS.md` as a
**second site**: a large project built under written authority with
executable rules, by the same author and the same assistant, in a domain
with no transactions in it, and **with no sentinel installed**. Not a second
implementation of the Sentinel Principle — it implements none of it — but a
place where the principle's predictions can be checked where it is absent.

The two repositories keep separate lifecycles and share no code. The
traffic between them is citation.

**The second site is private, and this file is public.** `milo-and-me` is a
closed repository and is to stay closed; the game in it is for demonstration
and not for distribution. So every citation below names a commit a reader of
this file cannot open. That is a real weakening and it is not repaired by
restating it: what follows is testimony about a place the reader cannot go,
offered by the pair who built it, and it is worth what such testimony is
worth. It is entered because the alternative — keeping a finding out of the
record because its site is closed — is the failure this repository exists to
refuse. The findings are stated so that they can be checked against THIS
repository's code, which is open, and two of the three already have been.

## What it makes true

**1. The pair that bounds what green can warrant.** `f7d0b61` found this
engine's harness measuring a stale binary: the questions were sound and the
subject was wrong, and the suite ran green against a target with no library
at all. The other site produced the dual. Its morph suite held a shape to
its creature kind for kind, station for station, swept a hundred points
between the two ends for a collapse, and proved every guard could fail by
reverting what it protected. All of it passed. The shape was a horse with
no legs, built out of an animal that has none to give, and it shipped —
because every assertion was about the specification and none was about
whether the thing was what it said it was.

> Right questions, wrong subject (here). Right subject, wrong questions
> (there). Each is sufficient for green, and neither is visible from
> inside the suite.

That is a sharper limit than this repository's README currently states.
The README says the principle does not show a verdict is correct. The pair
says something narrower and more useful: **a passage can be perfectly
governed and still carry something that was never what it claimed, because
a boundary checks the warrant it was given and not the claim it was given
it for.**

**2. Field evidence for `0004`, from a site that does not have it.** This
engine surfaces a disagreement between two namings of one fact as a row and
refuses to resolve it. The other site has no such mechanism, and in a single
working day the same shape appeared six times: a roster named in a table and
again in a hardcoded branch; an acceptance that walked four animals by hand
and asserted on four others by hand, reporting on one it had never stood in;
a vocabulary offered by an authoring page and a different one held by the
engine behind it; two builds of one game with no check that their casts
agreed; a morph bound to a creature its own file did not name. Each was
silent. Each was found by something other than the suite.

`0004` argues the remedy. This is the cost of not having it, measured
rather than argued, in a project that was otherwise well tested.

**3. A rule whose warrant is "look at it" is not a rule.** The other site
keeps a table of its own child-safety rules, each with the thing that holds
it. One row reads *no account, no network, no model at runtime; one offline
file*, and the column naming what holds it reads **the build itself**. The
build loads two scripts from two third-party content networks at run time.
The rule was written, published, and never crossed anything.

This is the Sentinel Principle's own sentence, observed where the principle
is not installed: an increase in a claim's authority that crossed no
independently governed boundary. The dates are the sharp part. The
dependency entered on 2026-09-28, the day the playtest was put in that
repository. The claim was written on 2026-10-05, a week later. It was found
on 2026-10-07 by a grep run while asking an unrelated question — two days
after it was written, and false on the day it was written.

## What it leaves, stated

- **Nothing here was run.** The three findings are read off another
  repository's commits and sources. Carrying them into this one's
  `TRANSFERS.md` is this author's act, and §5 of that file is where it is
  done. Nothing was re-run on either side to do it.
- **The dual is not symmetrical in cost.** `f7d0b61`'s remedy is cheap and
  named — carry the hash and mtime of the thing at the other end of the
  pipe. The other half has no cheap remedy. Asserting that an artefact is
  what it claims generally requires somebody to look at it, and both sites
  found their instance by looking. A talk that claims otherwise would be
  claiming more than either repository has.
- **Six is a count from one day, not a rate.** The drift instances were
  gathered while doing other work and are the ones that surfaced. The ones
  that did not surface are not counted, which is the whole difficulty.
- **The other site is not evidence about this engine.** It shares no code,
  no principal, no ledger and no harness. It is evidence about the method —
  written authority, executable rules, a dated record — and only that.
- **And it is unverifiable from here.** A reader cannot open the commits
  cited. Where a finding can be restated as a claim about this repository's
  own code it has been, and that part is checkable; the counts, the dates
  and the narrative are not. A talk that presents the second site's numbers
  as evidence should say they rest on the authors' word.
- **One of the three may still be this engine's.** `0004` already surfaces a
  two-naming disagreement. Whether a drift between two declarations that are
  never read together is the same thing as a disagreement between two
  namings that are, is a question this file raises and does not settle.

## Conflict, declared

The same assistant wrote the code at the other site, wrote its acceptances,
missed the legless horse through 215 of them, found it only when the author
looked at a film, and has now written the record of that failure and
proposed what it means. The author and the assistant are the same pair at
both sites. Agreement between them is worth what the opening
disclosure of `TRANSFERS.md` says such agreement is worth, and no more.

The check that does not rest on the assistant's premise is the one that
started it: the author watched the output and said the horse had no legs.
Nothing in either suite said so, before or after.
